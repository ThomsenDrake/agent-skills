## 1. Group announcement

A launch post to a channel of about 70 salespeople. A bot will start DMing them tomorrow.

**First draft (6 paragraphs, about 300 words)**, abridged:

> Hey team, heads up on something new from Beacon starting tomorrow morning.
>
> Beacon is going to start DMing you about meetings that look like they could be first meetings, meaning meetings at prospect accounts that don't have an open opp yet. Each card shows the meeting, the account and the contact, with two buttons: Yes and No.
>
> Yes tags the meeting in Salesforce under your own login, exactly like tagging it yourself. No marks it so Beacon stops asking about it. If you say Yes before the meeting, you'll get one more card right after it ends asking if it actually happened. Held moves it to Completed so it counts on the leaderboard, Didn't happen moves it to Cancelled.
>
> *(…timing, reminder schedule, how many cards to expect, which meetings it can't see, what to do if a card is wrong…)*
>
> Big thanks to Priya, Sam and Luis for piloting this the last few days. Questions in the thread 👇

**Over-cut rewrite (1 block, 99 words):**

> Heads up, starting tomorrow morning Beacon will DM you about meetings at prospect accounts with no open opp and ask if they're first meetings. Just hit Yes or No. Yes tags it in Salesforce for you, and you'll get a quick "did it happen?" card after the meeting so it counts. The first batch goes out around 7am CET / 1am ET and includes untagged meetings from last week. It only catches meetings linked to an account in Salesforce, so keep tagging anything else yourself. Thanks Priya, Sam and Luis for piloting, questions in the thread 👇

**The sender's own rewrite, proposed back to the agent (5 short paragraphs, about 150 words):**

> @here Hey team, just as a heads-up, starting tomorrow morning, a Slack app called Beacon will DM you about meetings at prospect accounts with no open opp and ask if they're first meetings.
>
> All you have to do is click Yes or No on each card that comes in. Yes tags it in Salesforce for you, and you'll get a quick "did it happen?" card after the meeting ends. Clicking that card is what makes it count for the leaderboard.
>
> The first batch goes out around 7am CET / 1am ET and includes untagged meetings from last week.
>
> Thanks @Priya, @Sam and @Luis for being my beta testers for this!
>
> Feel free to ask any questions in the replies or in a DM to me. I'll send a follow-up here once the first batch has all gone out!

The sender kept three of the rewrite's sentences almost word for word. They put back the greeting, paragraph breaks and the leaderboard reason. They added @here, real @mentions, what Beacon is, a DM option and a follow-up promise. They dropped the coverage caveat.

**The cut that broke a fact:** dropping "If you say Yes before the meeting" made every Yes sound like it gets a follow-up card. Most of the first batch was past meetings, which get no second card. Nobody noticed until a later review. Reread every sentence whose condition you cut.

## 2. Reply to a group thread

A kickoff reply to 10 volunteers for a tool pilot.

**First draft (177 words):**

> Thanks all! 10 volunteers, love it. Here's the plan for kicking off the pilot:
>
> *Working session: Fri Nov 20, 16:00–17:00 CET* (15:00 UK · 10:00 ET · 19:00 Gulf). I'll send an invite. It ends before the all-hands, so there's no clash. @Mei I'll DM you a separate slot that works from Australia.
>
> To make the hour count, please bring:
> 1. *One real account you're working on this week.* We'll run the tool on it live.
> 2. *The task from last week that took the longest or annoyed you most:* prospecting, account research, meeting prep, CRM updates, anything.
>
> Before Friday, if you haven't already: connect the plugin (Settings → Connectors, then set the tools to Allow) and skim the setup doc: https://example.com/setup. Credits aren't a concern, so experiment freely.
>
> Can't make Friday? Reply here with your top 3 time sinks and I'll set up 20 minutes with you 1:1.
>
> cc @Ana @Ben @Chloe @Dev @Eli @Fay @Gus @Hana @Ivo

**Over-cut rewrite (60 words):**

> Kicking off Fri Nov 20, 16:00–17:00 CET (10:00 ET), invite coming. Bring one real account and the most annoying task from your week, and we'll work through both live.
>
> @Mei I'll DM you a time that works from Australia. Can't make Friday? Reply here and we'll do 20 min 1:1.
>
> Setup doc if you want a head start: https://example.com/setup

The sender: "That might be too minimal. Can you find a middle ground here?"

**What the sender sent (about 150 words):**

> Thanks everyone for the great responses!
>
> Let's kick off with a working session on Fri Nov 20, 16:00–17:00 CET (15:00 UK / 10:00 ET). I'll send an invite shortly.
>
> Please bring two things:
> 1. One real account you're working on. We'll run the tool on it live.
> 2. The most annoying or time-consuming task from your week, even if it has nothing to do with prospecting. That's where the automation ideas come from.
>
> If you want a head start, connect the plugin (Settings → Connectors) and skim the setup doc: https://example.com/setup. Feel free to experiment before the session. We have plenty of credits, so don't worry about using them up.
>
> @Mei I'll DM you a time that works from Australia. If anyone can't make Friday, reply here and I'll find 20 min for a 1:1 with you.

The 60-word version never said what was kicking off, dropped the thanks, and turned "I'll send an invite" into "invite coming". The sender's version brings back the thanks, the event name, the numbered list, the reason for item 2, the setup step and the credits reassurance, and makes it longer. Both versions leave out the cc list, the no-clash line, the third time zone, the task examples and the "top 3 time sinks" ask. Those cuts held.

## 3. Message to stakeholders

A first post in a group DM with six stakeholders, sharing a prototype walkthrough video.

**First draft (289 words)**, abridged:

> Hi all, Sam from RevOps 👋 I work with Jo and Alex on our GTM systems and Salesforce.
>
> Following up on Alex's question last week about the interim partner referral app: we put together a rough v0 so there's something concrete to react to. Walkthrough here: https://example.com/walkthrough
>
> What it does today:
> • Partner view: the partner registers a deal and can see the status of what they've submitted
> • Our view: we review each registration and approve or reject it, and every decision is logged
> • It's built to push approved registrations into Salesforce, so Salesforce stays the source of truth
>
> *(…what it doesn't do yet, next steps, three paragraphs of questions for three people, an offer of a live walkthrough…)*

**Rewrite (87 words):**

> Hi all, Sam from RevOps 👋 Following up on Alex's question, I put together a rough v0 of the interim partner referral app: https://example.com/walkthrough
>
> @Omar yes, the partner registers the opp themselves and we review it on our side. It's on demo data for now, so it isn't connected to Salesforce or open to partners yet.
>
> @Lena @Theo does the flow match what you had in mind? @Ines happy to walk Paul and Kim through it so it fits with the portal work rather than duplicating it.

The agent's note: "I cut the feature list, next steps, Legal and pilot questions, and the offer of a live walkthrough. The video covers the features. The rest can come up in replies."

**What the sender posted (85 words):** almost the same text. They removed their name and team, attached the video file in place of the link, removed the tag on the first person, and scoped the answer: "In my prototype, yes, the partner registers the opp themselves…". About two hours later a stakeholder replied: "this looks amazing tbh, some fine tune will be needed but overall looks great", followed by a concrete feature request. The short post drew a real reply, which is the point of the site.

## 4. Request to another team

A request to a platform lead for capacity, plus service accounts.

**First draft (149 words, plus a 147-word thread of eight questions)**, abridged:

> Hi @Jordan, a heads-up and quota question for Atlas, an internal agent factory. Its workers run in the sandbox service.
>
> Plan:
> • Owner: a dedicated service account. Its key never enters a VM.
> • Every sandbox creator-only. Metadata labels, opaque IDs only.
> • Load: week 1 ≤5 concurrent; month 1 ≤20 at 4 CPU / 8 GiB / 16 GiB. TTL leases, idle pause 900 s.
> • Kill switch: list by metadata, per-sandbox DELETE, then checkpoints. Drilled weekly.
>
> Is that envelope OK, and should a quota name the principal? Questions in thread.

**Rewrite (94 words), with an "If they ask" list kept under the draft and not sent:**

> Hi @Jordan, heads-up: I'm building Atlas, an internal agent factory whose workers will run in the sandbox service. I expect up to 5 concurrent sandboxes this week and up to 20 (4 CPU / 8 GiB each) within a month. Does that work, or should I ask for a quota? Also, who can create two service accounts for it in the Main workspace? For context, I'm on the sales team's applied-AI work, and I sit in RevOps only because I was hired there before that team existed.
>
> **If they ask, or once they reply (one at a time, most useful first):** region pinning; the sanctioned way to make a sandbox private; batch delete; result retention; key rotation; the full plan.

**What the sender sent (65 words):** the same message without the product name and without the role sentence. That sentence was one the sender had asked for earlier. The reply came the same day: "no need to ask for quota… go for it". Requests to people who know you can go shorter than the rewrite. Context that you'd want to include "for the record" is usually the first thing to go.

## 5. Decision page

A page asking four reviewer teams to approve a pilot. Earlier the sender had said "That's way too much detail" about a 2,535-word chat answer, and the agent cut it to 705 words. Then the sender asked for the page, "not a slop grenade".

The page is not short (1,237 words). Its length follows the content: options, risks, open questions per team. What makes it not a slop grenade is the first screen:

> # Partner portal: approve the partner sign-in pilot
>
> 📌 **Ask:** approve a pilot of partner sign-in for up to 50 named users from 2 partner companies, Dec 1 to Jan 31. Without it, Dec 1 ships internal review only and partners keep emailing their registrations.
>
> **From you:** answer your team's rows in the Open questions table at the bottom, in the thread, by Nov 24 (platform team) or Nov 25 (everyone else).

Below that: what the app is (2 sentences and 2 bullets), why the default host can't be used (3 bullets), an options table with a verdict and a one-line reason per row, risks, and an open-questions table with one row per team. Citations sit in a collapsed "Evidence" section.

A fact-checking pass on the page restored qualifiers that compression had dropped ("draft rule", "low-priority", "no Security review yet") and fixed a version number. Long pages need that check as much as short messages do.

## What these show

| | Agent's cut | Sender's final | What the sender changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Announcement | 300 → 99 | about 150 | Added back the greeting, paragraphs, @mentions, the reason and a follow-up promise |
| 2. Group reply | 177 → 60 | about 150 | "Too minimal": added back the thanks, the event name, the list, the reasons and the reassurance |
| 3. Stakeholder message | 289 → 87 | 85 | Kept the cut. Scoped the "yes" and removed their own intro |
| 4. Request | 149 + thread → 94 | 65 | Cut further: the product name and the role sentence |
| 5. Decision page | Chat answer 2,535 → 705 ("way too much detail"), then a 1,237-word page | No reaction recorded | The ask and the reader's part on the first screen |

Requests and replies to people who know you can be cut hard. Posts to a group need their human parts back. Long docs are fine when the decision is on the first screen and every section is for a reader.
